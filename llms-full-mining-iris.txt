## Select a site and analysis
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/select-site/

# Select a site and analysis

Start in Iris 2.0 by choosing a site and the analysis you want to explore. You can browse demo sites or your own sites, then open the latest analysis or select an earlier analysis period.

## Find a site

1. On the **Select a Site** screen, choose **Demo Sites** to explore sample sites, or **My Sites** to browse your own sites.
2. Browse the site cards, or enter a site name in **Search sites**.
3. Click a site to see its available analyses.

## Choose an analysis type

A site can have analyses from ascending observations, descending observations, or a combination of both. The suffix in each analysis name identifies its type.

| Suffix | Analysis type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D** | Descending | Uses satellite observations from descending passes. |
| **A** | Ascending | Uses satellite observations from ascending passes. |
| **EWZ** | East–west and vertical | Combines ascending and descending observations using their viewing geometry to calculate east–west and vertical deformation components. |

For example, **Copler-failure-D**, **Copler-failure-A**, and **Copler-failure-EWZ** are different analysis types for the same site.

Once an analysis opens, its [map legend](analysis-legends.md) identifies the viewing geometry or deformation component being displayed.

## Choose an analysis period

Iris selects the most recent analysis by default. Click the analysis card to open it.

To open a different analysis when multiple periods are available:

1. Click the dropdown arrow beside **Last updated** on the analysis card.
2. Select the analysis period you want to open. Each option shows its start and end dates.

Analyses typically cover a one-year window. This captures longer-term deformation trends and provides enough observations to support measurements with high confidence.

Once the analysis opens, [change the map appearance](map-display.md) to choose a basemap and adjust the data opacity and colors.

## Read the analysis legend
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/analysis-legends/

# Read the analysis legend

The map legend has two parts: a direction symbol above the color bar identifies the viewing geometry or deformation component, and the color bar shows how the selected field's values map to colors.

## Line of sight analyses

Ascending and descending analyses show deformation along the satellite's line of sight. Their legends use diagonal viewing-direction symbols. Sentinel-1 is right-looking, and the symbol distinguishes the viewing geometry of the two orbit directions.

| Legend | Analysis | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | **Ascending LOS** | The diagonal arrow runs from the upper left toward the lower right. This symbol identifies the ascending viewing geometry and corresponds to an **A** analysis. |
|  | **Descending LOS** | The diagonal arrow runs from the upper right toward the lower left. This symbol identifies the descending viewing geometry and corresponds to a **D** analysis. |

LOS deformation measures movement along that viewing direction. It can contain horizontal and vertical contributions; use an east–west–vertical analysis to examine the separate components.

## East west and vertical components

An **EWZ** analysis combines ascending and descending observations to resolve east–west and vertical deformation. The symbol changes with the component displayed.

| Legend | Component | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | **Vertical** | The vertical arrow identifies the up–down component of deformation. |
|  | **East–west** | The horizontal arrow identifies the east–west component of deformation. |

**East–west sign convention:** Negative values represent **westward** movement; positive values represent **eastward** movement.

## Read the color bar

Read each color against its numeric value and the units printed above the bar. In these examples, **mm** means millimeters of deformation. The direction symbol identifies the viewing geometry or component; use the numeric scale to interpret the colored data.

The scale depends on the selected field and **Colorscale Range**. Changing the colormap or **Invert** changes the relationship between colors and values, so check the legend after making adjustments. See [Change the map appearance](map-display.md) for these controls.

## Choose a deformation field
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/data-fields/

# Choose a deformation field

Use the **Field** dropdown to choose the measurement displayed on the map. For ascending and descending analyses, you can view line-of-sight deformation, examine its uncertainty, or measure movement relative to a reference point you choose.

## Select a field

1. [Open a site and analysis](select-site.md) and wait for the data to load.
2. Open **Field** in the **Analysis** panel on the left.
3. Select the field you want to view and allow the display to update.

Check the map legend and chart axis labels after switching fields, because the quantity being displayed changes.

The [direction symbol above the color bar](analysis-legends.md) identifies the LOS viewing geometry or the east–west or vertical component of an EWZ analysis.

## LOS

**LOS** means **line of sight**: deformation measured along the satellite's viewing direction.

The **LOS** field uses spatial filtering to highlight local deformation. At each epoch, Iris fits a broad spatial trend to the data and subtracts it. The resulting deformation is relative to the surrounding deformation rather than to a single reference point you select.

Spatial filtering can also remove broad deformation signals. To investigate movement relative to a known location, use **LOS (Unfiltered)**. See [Spatial Filtering in Iris](https://mining.earthdaily.com/knowledge-base/knowledge/spatial-filtering-in-iris) for the processing explanation.

To focus the map on deformation of interest, [set the color range](map-display.md#set-the-color-range) and [hide smaller movements with a mask threshold](map-display.md#hide-smaller-movements).

## LOS Uncertainty

Select **LOS (Uncertainty)** to display the estimated error associated with each point. Use this field to assess the deformation relative to its uncertainty.

In **POINT** mode, select a map location to inspect how its uncertainty changes through the analysis period. The vertical axis identifies the chart as **LOS (Uncertainty) (mm)**.

You can also show uncertainty as error bars on a deformation chart using [Plot STD](point-time-series.md#show-measurement-uncertainty).

## LOS Unfiltered

Select **LOS (Unfiltered)** to use an **orange reference marker** as the spatial reference for the data. Spatial filtering is not applied in this field. All displayed deformation is relative to the orange marker's location.

1. Choose **LOS (Unfiltered)** from **Field**.
2. Drag the orange marker to your reference location, such as a station known to have remained stable over the analysis period.
3. Use **POINT** mode to select the location you want to inspect. Its time series shows movement relative to the orange reference point.
4. Move the orange marker to change the reference location. Allow the map and chart to update.

The **blue marker** identifies the point you are inspecting; the **orange marker** sets the reference. Choose a reference near the area of interest with reliable data and known stability over the analysis window.

This field is useful for comparing movement with a stable station or examining the difference between adjacent moving and stable areas. The reference point's own movement affects all values measured relative to it. See [Spatial Filtering in Iris](https://mining.earthdaily.com/knowledge-base/knowledge/spatial-filtering-in-iris) for more guidance on reference selection.

## Additional fields

The **Field** dropdown also provides the following options when available for the analysis:

- **LOS Velocity (180 days)**
- **LOS Acceleration (180 days)**
- **Coherence**
- **Estimated DEM Error**

For background on velocity, acceleration, and coherence, see [Data Layers in Iris](https://mining.earthdaily.com/knowledge-base/knowledge/data-layers-in-iris).

## Change the map appearance
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/map-display/

# Change the map appearance

Choose a basemap, set the color range, and hide smaller movements to focus on deformation of interest. Adjust the layer's opacity and palette to view the data alongside features on the ground.

## Open an analysis

[Select a site and analysis](select-site.md), then wait for the data to finish loading. Iris displays the analysis on the map, with **Analysis** controls on the left.

Use **Field** to [choose the deformation measurement](data-fields.md) before adjusting its appearance.

Read the [analysis legend](analysis-legends.md) to identify the viewing geometry or deformation component and interpret the color bar.

## Change the basemap

The basemap provides geographic context beneath the deformation data.

1. Click the **stacked layers icon** on the right side of the map to open the **Basemap** panel.
2. Click a basemap thumbnail to apply it. Options include **Satellite**, **Streets**, and **Outdoors**. Scroll within the panel to see more options.
3. Click **×** in the panel's upper-right corner to close it.

Use **Satellite** to see imagery of the site, or try **Streets** or **Outdoors** for a map background.

## Set the color range

Use **Colorscale Range** in the **Analysis** panel to set the minimum and maximum values represented by the color bar.

1. Enter a minimum in the left value box and a maximum in the right value box, or drag the two slider handles.
2. Allow the map to update.
3. Check the color legend on the map to see the new value-to-color mapping.

For **LOS**, the values are in millimeters. You can set a symmetric range around zero or use different limits on either side. In the example below, the minimum is **−100 mm** and the maximum is **50 mm**.

Changing this range changes the colors used to display deformation. Use **Mask Threshold (+/-)** to hide points with smaller movement magnitudes.

## Hide smaller movements

Use **Mask Threshold (+/-)** to hide points whose deformation magnitude is below an absolute threshold. The threshold applies to both positive and negative values.

1. Find **Mask Threshold (+/-)** below **Colorscale Range**.
2. Enter a threshold in the value box, or adjust the slider.
3. Allow the map to update to show the points that pass the threshold.

For example, set the threshold to **20** when viewing **LOS** to focus on movement greater than **20 mm** in magnitude. Points with **−30 mm** or **30 mm** of deformation remain visible, while points with **−10 mm** or **10 mm** are hidden.

Set the threshold back to **0** to show smaller movements again.

## Adjust the data opacity

Use **Colormap Opacity** in the **Analysis** panel to control how strongly the deformation layer covers the basemap.

1. Find the **Colormap Opacity** slider on the left.
2. Drag it left to make the data more transparent, or right to make it more opaque. The percentage beside the slider shows the current setting.

Lower the opacity when you want to see roads, terrain, or other features beneath the data. Increase it when you want the data colors to stand out. At **100%**, the data layer is fully opaque where data is displayed.

## Change the colormap

The colormap assigns colors to the values shown on the map.

1. Open the **Colormap** dropdown in the **Analysis** panel.
2. Select a palette, such as **Jet**, **Viridis**, **Magma**, **RdYlBu**, or **Spectral**.
3. To reverse the order of the colors, toggle **Invert** beside the dropdown.

Use the color legend on the map to check which values each color represents, especially after changing the palette or toggling **Invert**.

To investigate a location in more detail, [inspect and compare point deformation](point-time-series.md).

## Point
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/point-time-series/

# Inspect and compare point deformation

Select a location on the map to view its deformation over time. To compare locations, add up to three points to the same time series chart.

## View a point time series

1. [Open a site and analysis](select-site.md) and wait for the data to load.
2. In the **Time Series** toolbar beneath the map, click **POINT**.
3. Click a location on the map where deformation data is available.

Iris places a marker at the selected location and displays its time series beneath the map. The chart shows time along the horizontal axis and deformation along the vertical axis. Check the vertical axis label for the measurement and units; the example below shows **LOS (mm)**.

## Move the selected point

With **Compare points** switched off, click another location on the map to move the point, or drag its marker to a new location. The chart updates to show the time series for the new location.

Use this to explore how deformation varies across the site.

To mark locations you want to revisit, [create a Measurement Sites vector layer](vector-layers.md#create-measurement-site-points).

## Show measurement uncertainty

Select **Plot STD** in the **Time Series** toolbar to display standard deviation as error bars on the deformation measurements. Clear the checkbox to hide them.

Compare the size of the deformation with the error bars to assess the movement relative to its measurement uncertainty.

## Compare up to three points

1. Select your first point using **POINT** mode.
2. Switch on **Compare points** in the **Time Series** toolbar.
3. Click another location on the map to add a second point.
4. Click a third location if you want to compare three points.

Iris displays the points together on the chart. Match each series to its map marker using the colors and the **Point 1**, **Point 2**, and **Point 3** labels.

The limit is **three points in total**. To remove a point, click **×** beside its name in the toolbar. You can then click the map to add another location.

To examine deformation along a line, [view a transect profile](transect-profiles.md).

To summarize deformation across a region, [view an area time series](area-time-series.md) or [inspect an anomaly polygon](deformation-anomalies.md).

To download the displayed measurements, [export the current plot as CSV](export-data.md#export-the-current-plot).

## Transect
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/transect-profiles/

# View deformation along a transect

Draw a line across the site to view a deformation profile. Step through the analysis dates to see how deformation develops along the same line over time.

## Draw a transect

1. [Open a site and analysis](select-site.md) and wait for the data to load.
2. In the **Time Series** toolbar beneath the map, click **TRANSECT**.
3. Draw a line on the map along the area you want to investigate.
4. Wait for the profile to load beneath the map.

The chart now shows **distance along the transect in meters** on the horizontal axis and **deformation** on the vertical axis. Check the vertical axis label for the measurement and units; the example below shows **LOS (mm)**.

The profile displays deformation for the **end date** selected under **Date Range**. A point time series plots one location through time; a transect profile plots locations along a line at the selected end date.

Select **Plot STD** to show standard deviation as error bars along the profile. Use these to assess deformation relative to its measurement uncertainty.

## Follow the profile through time

Keep the transect in place and change the end date to examine how deformation develops along it.

1. With the full analysis period selected, select **Lock Y-Axis** in the **Time Series** toolbar. This keeps the chart's current vertical limits fixed.
2. In the **Analysis** panel, find **Date Range**. Keep the start date fixed and drag the right-hand slider handle back to the earliest available end date, or epoch 0.
3. Use the right arrow beside the end-date field to advance through the available dates. You can also drag the end-date handle forward to move through the period.
4. Allow the map and profile to finish updating at each date before comparing the next one.

At each step, the map and distance profile update for the selected end date. With the y-axis locked, you can compare deformation magnitudes on a consistent vertical scale throughout the analysis window.

Clear **Lock Y-Axis** when you want the chart to scale automatically again.

## Area
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/area-time-series/

# View average deformation within an area

Draw a polygon or select an existing polygon on the map to view the average deformation within its boundary over time.

## Draw an area

1. [Open a site and analysis](select-site.md) and wait for the data to load.
2. In the **Time Series** toolbar beneath the map, click **AREA**.
3. Draw and complete a polygon around the area you want to investigate.
4. Wait for the area time series to load beneath the map.

Iris highlights the polygon and plots the average deformation of the data points inside it. The horizontal axis shows time; the vertical axis shows deformation. Check the axis label for the measurement and units.

## Select an existing polygon

Use **SELECT FEATURE** to inspect an area that already has a polygon on the map. For example, you can select an automatically calculated [deformation anomaly](deformation-anomalies.md).

You can also [draw or import polygon vector layers](vector-layers.md) to define your own boundaries.

1. Make the polygon layer visible. For anomaly polygons, turn on **Current Anomalies** in the **Anomalies** panel.
2. Click **SELECT FEATURE** in the **Time Series** toolbar.
3. Click the polygon you want to inspect on the map.
4. Wait for the area time series to update.

Iris highlights the selected polygon and shows the average deformation within its boundary. To inspect a different area, click **SELECT FEATURE** again and select another polygon.

## Investigate anomalies
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/deformation-anomalies/

# Investigate deformation anomalies

Iris automatically identifies anomalous deformation and outlines the affected regions as polygons. Use these outlines to locate areas for closer inspection and view their average deformation over time.

## Show current anomalies

1. [Open a site and analysis](select-site.md) and wait for the data to load.
2. Click the **diamond-shaped exclamation icon** in the left toolbar to open **Anomalies**.
3. Select **Current Anomalies** to display the anomaly polygons for the latest acquisition in the analysis.

Use the **Anomaly Type** legend on the map to identify the categories. Clear **Current Anomalies** to hide the outlines.

## Understand anomaly types

| Type | Map color | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Steady** | Green | A linear deformation trend during the preceding 120 days, with velocity exceeding 0.05 mm/day. |
| **Sudden** | Yellow | A statistically anomalous rise in velocity during the most recent four acquisitions. |
| **Complex** | Magenta | Steady and sudden deformation behavior occurring within one contiguous area. |

These categories identify rapid or unusual deformation and changes from historical trends. They do not predict future motion or establish the overall stability of an area. See the existing [Deformation anomalies documentation](https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/using-iris/adding-anomalies/) for these definitions and [Anomaly Detection in Iris](https://mining.earthdaily.com/knowledge-base/knowledge/anomaly-detection) for methodology.

## Inspect an anomaly polygon

1. With the anomaly outlines visible, click **SELECT FEATURE** in the **Time Series** toolbar beneath the map.
2. Click an anomaly polygon.
3. Wait for its area time series to load.

The selected polygon is highlighted, and the chart shows average deformation within its boundary over time. See [View average deformation within an area](area-time-series.md) for the full area workflow.

To use the anomaly boundaries in other software, [export the polygons as GeoJSON](export-data.md#export-anomaly-polygons).

## Draw and import vectors
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/vector-layers/

# Draw and import vector layers

Use vector layers to mark measurement locations and define areas on the map. You can draw points, lines, and polygons, or import features from a GeoJSON file.

## Open vector layers

1. [Open a site and analysis](select-site.md).
2. Click the **stacked layers icon in the left toolbar** to open **Layers**.
3. Select the **Vectors** tab.

Drawings are stored locally in your browser and may be lost if you clear its cache or cookies.

## Create measurement site points

A named point layer helps you return to the same measurement locations when reviewing deformation at different times.

1. Click **+ New** and choose **Point Layer**.
2. Rename the layer **Measurement Sites**, or give it another descriptive name.
3. Click **+** on the layer card to add features.
4. Click each location on the map where you want a measurement point.
5. Click **+** again to leave drawing mode.

The layer card shows the number of points you have added.

To inspect a measurement location, select **POINT** in the **Time Series** toolbar and click its location on the map. Iris displays the deformation time series for that location. Use the same marked locations when returning to your measurements. See [Inspect and compare point deformation](point-time-series.md) for point chart controls.

## Import GeoJSON features

Use **Import GeoJSON** in an empty vector panel, or **Import** at the top of the **Layers** panel, to load a `.geojson` file. Select the file containing the features you want to add.

Importing existing features lets you use predefined measurement locations or boundaries alongside your deformation analysis.

## Draw an export AOI

Create a polygon to define an **area of interest (AOI)** for an export.

1. In **Layers → Vectors**, click **+ New** and choose **Polygon Layer**.
2. Rename the layer **Export AOI**.
3. Click **+** on its layer card to enter drawing mode.
4. Draw and complete a polygon around the area you want to export.
5. Leave drawing mode when you have finished.

The polygon appears on the map, and its layer card shows the polygon count. Choose this layer in the Export panel to [export analysis data within the AOI](export-data.md#export-a-polygon-aoi).

You can also [select an existing polygon](area-time-series.md#select-an-existing-polygon) to view the average deformation within its boundary.

## Export data and results
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/v2/export-data/

# Export analysis data and results

Use the **Export** panel to download analysis points, the data in the current plot, or deformation anomaly polygons.

| Export | Format | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spatial data** | GeoParquet (`.parquet`) | Filtered or unfiltered analysis data for the full analysis or a polygon AOI. |
| **Current plot** | CSV (`.csv`) | Data from the plot currently displayed in Iris. |
| **Anomalies** | GeoJSON (`.geojson`) | Deformation anomaly polygons for the analysis. |

## Open the Export panel

1. [Open a site and analysis](select-site.md).
2. Click the **download arrow icon** in the left toolbar to open **Export**.

## Export spatial data

1. Under **Spatial data (.parquet)**, choose the **Deformation series** to include: **Filtered**, **Unfiltered**, or both.
2. Open **Area** and select **Entire analysis** to export the full analysis, or choose a polygon layer to restrict the export to an AOI.
3. Enter your preferred name in **File name**. The output uses the `.parquet` extension shown beside the field.
4. Click **Export spatial data**.
5. Wait for Iris to prepare the export and complete the download.

The download contains analysis points in **GeoParquet** format. Open it in GIS software with GeoParquet support. See the [GeoParquet software list](https://geoparquet.org/#software) for compatible tools.

For background on spatial filtering, see [Choose a deformation field](data-fields.md).

## Export a polygon AOI

To export a specific area, first [draw or import a polygon layer](vector-layers.md). In the example below, the polygon layer is named **Export AOI**.

1. In **Export → Spatial data (.parquet)**, select the deformation series you want.
2. Set **Area** to **Export AOI**, or the name of your polygon layer.
3. Enter a file name and click **Export spatial data**.

Iris clips the spatial export to the selected polygon area.

## Export the current plot

1. Display the plot you want to download, such as a [point time series](point-time-series.md), [area average](area-time-series.md), or [transect profile](transect-profiles.md).
2. Open **Export**.
3. Under **Current plot (.csv)**, click **Export plot data**.

Iris downloads the data from the current plot as a CSV file.

## Export anomaly polygons

1. Open **Export** for the analysis you want.
2. Under **Anomalies (.geojson)**, check the displayed polygon count.
3. Click **Export anomalies** to download the anomaly vectors as GeoJSON.

See [Investigate deformation anomalies](deformation-anomalies.md) for the steady, sudden, and complex categories.

## What is Iris?

At the core of Iris lies the innovative use of Interferometric Synthetic
Aperture Radar (InSAR) technology. This groundbreaking technology transcends
geographical boundaries to monitor assets consistently and comprehensively.

With its seamless integration of cutting-edge technology, Iris is an
indispensable tool for proactive risk management, precise decision-making, and
the preservation of critical assets.

### **Working with Iris**

Iris allows you to quickly and interactively access deformation over assets of
interest, allowing you to make quick decisions regarding the stability and
health of infrastructure and terrain.

### Fast

Iris utilizes data created in our state-of-the-art InSAR pipeline, the
only automated commercial InSAR pipeline, allowing speedy delivery and
the ability to scale to monitor hundreds of sites very quickly.

### Streamlined

All the tools you need are all in a single, easy to use interface. No more
switching between applications or working with complex bits of code.

### Global

Sentinel-1 global coverage allows users to have continuous monitoring anywhere
on the planet, or to run one-off analyses to understand where and when motion
may be occurring.

### **Getting started with Iris**

### New to InSAR?

If you're new to the world of InSAR, learn about some basics to understand
how active-source measurements taken from space can resolve mm-scale ground
motions, even when it's cloudy or dark out.

### Looking to jump in?

Hit the ground running by accessing Iris, adding a demo or customer analysis,
and querying points and areas to discover deformation and anomolous motion.

### Ready to keep learning?

Follow along in some of our case studies to see different examples of
how motion can be tracked, as well as interpretation tips and caveats.

## Header functions
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/using-iris/header/
Keywords: Iris header, bookmarks, basemaps, pixel inspector, zoom controls, navigation, Iris interface

## The Iris header bar

The header bar in Iris contains several tools for adding baselayers,
manipulating the map, and links to further information on Iris itself.

### **Bookmarks**

Bookmarks are a convenient way to store locations that you will return to often.
Each bookmark is defined by a latitude/longitude location and a zoom level.
Clicking on a bookmark will move the map to the defined center and zoom. Click
`Create a bookmark` to add the current map location as a bookmark, or click
`Manage bookmarks` to review and manage your current bookmarks.

### **Basemaps**

Basemaps provide different images underlying the deformation points to
help interpret what you're seeing on the map. These include free optical
imagery, shaded relief, OpenStreetMap, and more.

### **Zoom and center**

Controls the center latitude/longitude value and zoom level of the map.

### **Pixel inspector**

The pixel inspector will extract the pixel values for currently visualized
layers that are from Optical Imagery and Derived Layers. Click the icon to
start an inspector session, and click on the map to extract a point.
Clicking the map in another location will move the inspection point and
recompute the values shown in the box.

### **Changelog**

Click this icon to bring up the release notes for the most recent Iris
version.

### **Help**

The help icon links to this documentation.

## Adding deformation layers

Keywords: deformation layers, InSAR visualization, Iris, AOI selection, ascending track, descending track, EarthDaily

## Add deformation layers for visualization

Click the plus sign next to Deformation layers and select the method to choose AOIs to load.

### **Add a deformation layer via Map view**

When you select `GDS layer (map)`, the map in the visualization window is updated with markers to show AOIs available to the user.
**Demo** sites are indicated by orange markers and **Customer** sites by blue markers. The icon of the marker indicates the imaging geometry of the stack used for the analysis: An up arrow to indicate an ascending track, a down arrow to indicate a descending track and a cross to indicate East-West-Up analysis.

The image below is representative and the actual sites available to the user may differ.

Click on an icon to open up the detailed view of the AOI. In this view, pick an analysis spanning
the time period of interest from the drop down labeled `Select analysis` and hit `Add layer` to
load the analysis.

## Deformation map settings
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/using-iris/visualization-settings/map-settings/
Keywords: map settings, InSAR visualization, colormap, mask threshold, time slider, Iris, deformation display

## Map settings

Settings to control what the InSAR points look like on the map.

The eyeball icon allows the points on the map to be toggled on and off without needing to remove the analysis fully.

### **Layer**

This dropdown allows you to choose the layer you would like to display. For ascending or descending analyses, this will default to **LOS**, which stands for line of site.

    More information about Sentinel-1 InSAR, including look direction, can be found [here](https://hyp3-docs.asf.alaska.edu/guides/insar_product_guide/#brief-overview-of-insar).

The incidence angle is plotted at the top right of the map for easy interpretation of direction of motion.

If the analysis is an east-west-vertical analysis, obtained by combining ascending and descending, the default will be **Up (+) Down (-)**. By selecting the dropdown, you can choose a different layer to display on the map. For line of sight analyses, you will typically find **LOS Uncertainty**, **LOS (Unfiltered)**, **LOS Velocity (180 days)**, **LOS Acceleration (180 days)**, **Coherence**, and in some cases, **Estimated DEM error**. For east-west-vertical analyses, the options will typically include **Up (+) Down (-)**, **Vertical Uncertainty**, **Up (+) Down (-) (Unfiltered)**, **Vertical Velocity (180 days)**, **Vertical Acceleration (180 days)**, **East (+) West (-)**, **East/West Uncertainty**, **East (+) West (-) (Unfiltered)**, **East Velocity (180 days)**, and **East Acceleration (180 days)**. Changing these layers will both change which points are overlain on the map, and will update what is being plotted on the plot below the map. For more information about settings for the plot below the map, visit [time-series settings](./time-series-settings.md).

### **Min & Max**

The Min and Max values control the colorbar being used to plot the data. Changing these values can be useful for highlighting areas of high amounts of motion. For line of sight analyses, negative motion is motion away from the satellite (e.g. subsidence), and positive motion is motion towards the satellite (e.g. uplift). For east-west-vertical analyses, negative motion is down or west, and positive motion is up or east. This information is provided in the name of the layer.

Changes to the min & max values will be reflected in the colorbar on the bottom right of the map. Numbers can be typed in or changed incrementally using the arrows.

### **Colormap**

The colormap dropdown allows you to change what color the points are plotted on the map. It defaults to **jet**. Changing this will change the colors on the map and the colorbar.

### **Invert colormap**

The **Invert colormap** button flips the colormap for the layer, while maintaining the same min and max values.

### **Mask Threshold (+/-)**

The mask threshold allows you to mask out points below a certain value. This is helpful for quickly identify large areas of motion, while ignoring smaller areas of motion. Numbers can be typed in or changed incrementally using the arrows.

### **Time slider**

Below the **Mask Threshold** is the **Time Slider**, which allows users to adjust both the start and end date of the analysis. The cumulative deformation over the given time window will be plotted on the map.

### **Opacity**

The **Opacity** slider allows the user to change the transparency of the points on the map. 1 is the default, and 0 is fully transparent. This can be useful to easily see what is below the points on the map without having to fully toggle on and off the layer.

## Time series plot settings
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/using-iris/visualization-settings/time-series-settings/
Keywords: time series, measurement type, transect, area plot, historical data, Iris, repositioned points, standard deviation

## Time series settings

Controls for data displayed on the time series.

### **Measurement type**

In Iris, you have the ability to plot **Point**, **Area**, or **Transect**. **Point** is the default, and will correspond to the data at the green pointer on the map. To select points, simply drag the pointer to a new location on the map.

**Area** will compute the average area and 2 times the standard deviation over a chosen polygon. To display area, toggle the radio button to the **Area** label, then select polygons using the **Vector Layer** dropdown, which will display the available polygons you have drawn or loaded into the session. Refer to the [Vector layers](../vector-layers.md) page for instructions on adding/creating vectors.

**Transects** will display the data along a transect. The data is from the end epoch selected with the time slider (see [Map settings](map-settings.md) for more information), meaning it will be consistent with what is displayed on the map. The vertical axis will remain as deformation in mm, while the horizontal axis will now be meters along the transect. To display the transect, toggle the radio button to the **Transect** label, then select polylines using the **Vector Layer** dropdown, which will display the available polylines you have drawn or loaded into the session. Refer to the [Vector layers](../vector-layers.md) page for instructions on adding/creating vectors.

    To see the exact location a point is on the map, you can hover over the transect data plotted and a blue pointer will show you the location on the transect.

### **Plot STD**

Clicking the `Plot STD` checkbox will plot the standard deviation for the layer, if it applies to the layer. If it does not apply to the selected layer, the checkbox will be greyed out.

### **Lock y-axis**

The lock y-axis button locks the time series to its current limits, instead of scaling the y-axis as you select different points to display. This can be particularly useful when comparing magnitudes of different points, or to watch the development of deformation over time along a transect.

### **Show historical**

This checkbox can be toggled on and off to look at the historical statistics of the data being plotted, if previous analyses exist. The historical analysis is the mean of all other analyses that were run that included this particular point.

    To read more about DEM-error corrected points in Iris, check out our Knowledge Base article [here](https://mining.earthdaily.com/knowledge-base/knowledge/historical-consistency).

If `Show historical` is checked as well as `Plot STD`, the standard deviation for the current analysis is calculated from only this analysis, while the historical standard deviation is calculated from all of the other analyses included for this point. If no historical analyses exist for a dataset, the box will be greyed out.

### **Pixel**

This box gives the exact pixel location of the point you have selected in lat/long coordinates.

### **Use repositioned points**

The **Use repositioned points** button allows the points to be displayed either using their regularly-gridded locations at the given posting, our with point locations that have been corrected for DEM-errors.

    To read more about DEM-error corrected points in Iris, check out our Knowledge Base article [here](https://mining.earthdaily.com/knowledge-base/knowledge/dem-error-corrected-point-locations).

The default behavior is to use the repositioned points. If DEM-error corrected points do not exist for a dataset, the box will be greyed out.

## Deformation anomalies
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/using-iris/adding-anomalies/
Keywords: deformation anomalies, anomaly detection, InSAR monitoring, Iris, ground movement, anomaly persistence, time series

## Deformation anomalies

The goal of **anomaly detection** is to identify behaviors that may indicate potential issues, and to identify regions where there has been a change from the historical trend that may require attention. These trends are meant to easily highlight areas that are deforming rapidly or anomalously, but do not predict any type of future motion or the overall stability of an area.

    To read more about deformation anomalies in Iris, check out our Knowledge Base article [here](https://mining.earthdaily.com/knowledge-base/knowledge/anomaly-detection).

Click the checkboxes in the left panel to add anomalies to the current analysis. There three are ways to visualize deformation anomalies.

    Multiple types of anomalies can be plotted at the same time

### **Current anomalies**

Current anomalies are areas for the latest collect in the analysos that are classified as one of three types (see legend):

1. Steady: deformation with a linear trend over the last 120 days and a velocity above 0.05 mm/day
2. Sudden: deformation with statistically anomalous increase in velocity within the last 4 collects
3. Complex: both steady and sudden behavior within a contigious area

### **Anomaly persistence**

Regardless of type, anomalies for all collects are plotted with a low opacity. Therefore, high opacity areas have more persistant anomalous deformation than low-opacity features.

### **Anomaly time series**

Anomalies for all collects are plotted, colored by date. This allows users to see how features have changed over time.

## Adding vector layers
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/using-iris/vector-layers/
Keywords: vector layers, GeoJSON upload, shapefile, draw polygon, Iris, spatial data, GeoPackage

## Add vector layers

Iris supports several methods for adding vector layers to your current viewer.

### **Draw a vector layer**

Select `Draw layer` to create a vector layer by manually drawing one on the map.

The dialog allows you to type a name for the vector and select the geometry
type. Available options are `Polygon`, `Polyline`, and `Points`. After clicking
the `Add layer` button, the new vector will appear in the layer list. Click the
[draw](index.md#toggle-drawing) icon to toggle drawing, then click the new icon
on the map to begin the drawing session.

    The icon on the map will indicate whether the drawn layer will be a polygon,
    point, or polyline.

Click on the map to draw the desired vector object.

- For polygons, finalize the polygon by clicking the first point to close the
  polygon.
- For polylines, finalize by clicking the last point.
- For points, each individual point will be drawn separately.

### **Upload a GeoJSON layer**

Select the `Upload vector` option to load a vector layer from your local
computer. Valid formats for loading vectors include:

- GeoJSONs
- Shapefiles (as `.zip` archives)
- GeoPackage (`.gpkg`)
- CSVs
- Parquet
- AutoCAD DXF
- KML/KMZ

    Vector files have a large amount of variability from one software package to the
    next. Iris attempts to load vectors using the
    [GeoPandas](https://geopandas.org/en/stable/docs/reference/api/geopandas.read_file.html)
    package, which is successful for most vector files from common sources (QGIS,
    Arc, etc). For help loading non-standard vector layers, contact 
    [support](mailto:support@earthdaily.com).

### **Load from S3**

This is a special option that loads specific polygons associated with customizations requested for a customer site. This option can
only be used after a [deformation layer](./adding-deformation-layers.md) has been added for visualization.
Users will have the option to choose the project, the type of polygons (exclusion or inclusion) and whether to apply it to the current analysis to mask the analysis or alerts.

## Export data
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/using-iris/export-data/
Keywords: export data, download parquet, CSV export, Iris, InSAR data, deformation analysis

## Export data

Data available in iris can be downloaded for use in other software. To download data, scroll to the bottom of the left panel and click the save icon next to the `Export Layer` label.

### **Download .parquet file**

To download the entire analysis as a parquet file, first select the layers you would like (`Filtered`, `Unfiltered`, and/or `Repositioned points`), then select the area you would like under the `Export area` dropdown. If you choose `Entire analysis`, all of the points will be downloaded. Next, click on the `Generate Download` button. This will create a link that you can click on to start your download. **MGRS**, **EASTING**, **NORTHING**, **VEL**, **ACC**, **STDDEV**, **ALERT**, **COHERENCE**, **LAT**, **LON**, and **geometry** will be downloaded as well as the layers you have selected, when available.

    If you'd like to download a different set of data, click the `Generate Download` button a second time to recreate the download link.

### **Download current plot**

To download the current data shown on the plot, toggle the button to the `Current plot` option. Selecting `Generate Download` will automatically download a .csv file containing whatever data is currently on the plot. If point data is selected, the latitude and longitude will be included in the file name. If a transect or area is selected, the name of the polyline/polygon will be included in the file name. All data shown on the map, including deformation, historical data, standard deviation, etc. will be included in the output, with the exception of velocity and acceleration. In the case of point and area plots, dates will be included for each point. In the case of transect data, the latitude and longitude of each point will be included.

## Running quicklook
URL: https://docs.earthdaily.com/mining/iris/using-iris/running-quicklook/
Keywords: quicklook analysis, InSAR time series, Iris, Sentinel-1, burst footprints, interferogram, deformation processing

## Running Quicklook analysis

The **Quicklook analysis** tool in **Iris** can be used to quickly generate InSAR time-series over small AOIs interest. The quicklook analysis is very useful in understanding the spatial distribution of reliable scatterers over an AOI and also helps identify key deforming features quickly, when possible. Our quicklook analysis builds on our Global InSAR layer which includes all Sentinel-1 burst interferograms with a temporal baseline less than 24 days.

    Quicklook Analysis is only available to qualified customers with platform access.

### **Use stack analysis mode**

Select `Stack Viewer` tool from the `Stack analysis` header on the left panel.

This should change the appearance of the main map area to look like this.

    The actual buttons available to the user will depend on the subscription privileges.

### **Search for Sentinel-1 burst footprints**

The tool is set up to analyze the last one year of data by default. The user can modify the time period of interest as needed. Zoom over the AOI of interest and hit the `Footprints` button to look for InSAR data overlapping the area of interest in the given time period. This should update the map view as shown below:

The original search area is shown as black rectangle and is overlaid by polygons representing the Sentinel-1 burst footprints intersecting the search area. User is able to click on the polygons and the timeline plot under the map is updated with a plot of the interferogram network for analysis. This allows the user to select the imaging geometry with best temporal sampling and coverage of their AOI for the quicklook analysis.
After clicking on the burst footprint to use for analysis, hit the `Quicklook` button to open the `Run Quicklook analysis` dialog.

    If the selected AOI is too large, selection of burst footprints could fail. If the available interferogram network is not connected, Quicklook analysis may not be available over the AOI.

### **Verify parameters**

The `Run quicklook analysis` dialog should look like above. The user can set or verify the following parameters:

1. **AOI:** If the user has vector polygons available, they can choose a more specific polygon compared to the original search area.
2. **Stitching bursts:** If the user's AOI is not entirely covered by a single burst footprint, they can opt to stitch bursts along track to cover the AOI.
3. **Name:** Pick a meaningful name for the analysis. This field is mandatory.
4. **Resolution:** The global InSAR layer has a posting of 20m. For larger AOIs, we suggest using value of 40m or 80m to speed up the analysis.
5. **Coherence threshold:** This parameter controls the selection of a coherent reference region for time-series estimation.
6. **Quality threshold:** This parameter is similar to temporal coherence for pixel selection, prior to phase unwrapping.

At this stage, the user has an option to estimate the number of points that could potentially be generated by the chosen set of parameters.

### **Launch the analysis and monitor progress**

When ready, hit the `Submit` button and this should start a compute job on the platform. You can check the progress on the [Compute services page](https://earthone.earthdaily.com/compute).

On completion of the processing job, the results are written to the [Storage service](https://earthone.earthdaily.com/storage) and should be available for visualization. The processing time depends on the size of the AOI and the number of usable interferograms in the time period of interest. A typical 5 km x 5km AOI takes about 10 minutes to process for 1 year's worth of data.

### **Visualizing results**

The results should be available to visualize just like [other deformation layers](./adding-deformation-layers.md).

## Global velocity viewer

The **Global velocity viewer** is a tool to work with our **Global InSAR Velocity Layer** within Iris.

### **Usage**

To query points, click on the map to view the velocity data for the selected point. Velocities represent the average velocity over the calendar year displayed. To get exact values, toggle over the points.

### Starting and ending year

To update the starting and ending year, use the respective dropdowns to update what will be plotted on the time series, and, depending on settings, what will be shown on the map.

### Show imagery on map

Toggle the `Show imagery on map` button to quickly remove the data to view layers underneath. To add the Global Velocity Layer back to the map, simply check the box again.

### Map display

The data on the map can be displayed as just the velocity over the year chosen as the end date, or the cumulative mean over the time period. Looking over the most recent year can be useful for understanding recent trends, while looking at the cumulative values over the entire selected time period can be useful for highlighting long-term trends.

### Search tracks

In some locations, there will be overlapping orbit tracks. When multiple points are present for a specific date, this indicates overlapping orbits are present. The orbit tracks are each processed separately, so it is recommended that the data on the map be visualized separately.

To do so, click on the `Search tracks` button, which will populate the dropdown below. Click on a particular orbit track to view data only from this track. Orbit tracks are labeled in the legend of the velocity vs year plot.

## Tailings pond viewer

The **Tailings pond viewer** is a tool to interact with measurements of tailings pond levels. This tool is useful for understanding how much water is currently stored within a tailings pond, as well as how this has changed over time, which can be useful for understanding the health of the pond, meeting regulations, and understanding production levels.

    Water monitoring is only available for customers with an appropriate license. Contact support@earthdaily.com to discuss monitoring pond water at your sites!

### **Usage**

### Accessing tailings pond water

To access the tailings pond viewer, on the lefthand side, under **Water**, select **Tailings pond water**. If you have InSAR layers open at this time, you will be asked to confirm you would like to switch modes before proceeding.

From here, you can select on your minesite of interest, and then select which pond you would like to monitor. When you select the minesite, you will be taken there on the map.

### Plotting most recent water

To see the most recent water on the map, click the **Plot most recent pond water** button. This will plot the most recent water derived from cloud-free imagery. This will also create a polygon under your vector layers with the date the measurement was taken that can be downloaded as a GeoJSON.

    Since water monitoring requires cloud-free imagery, it is possible that the most recent measurements will have occurred a few weeks prior.

### Plotting year-long history

To plot the year-long history, select the **Plot year-long pond water**. This will plot the polygons from the past year on the map, and the percentage of the pond that was water on the plot below. To see specific dates and values, hover of points on the plot.
