## Recovering Work with Autosave
URL: https://earthdaily.github.io/documentation/mining/using-marigold/avoiding-lost-work-in-marigold/

# Recovering work in Marigold with the Autosave feature

## Gone in the blink of an eye? Chances are your hard work can be saved.

Periodically saving your Marigold workspace is the best way to ensure that your layers and products stay just the way you want them. Occasionally though, your session may close without saving. In many cases you can recover your work using Marigold's autosave feature.

### Marigold has closed without saving

This primarily happens due to timing-out. Marigold sessions are programmed to time-out after an hour of inactivity. You may return to your computer to see a notification that your connection to the server has been lost.

Don't panic! Simply click **Refresh** and Marigold will reload into a new session. To recover your work:

1. Click **Load** from the top panel menu
2. Select `autosave.sav` and click **Load Project**
3. Click **Save** from the top panel menu and name your saved file

### Tips and Considerations

- The autosave feature only works on one Marigold session at a time. While Marigold supports a user working in separate instances simultaneously, the `autosave.sav` file will only be associated with one.
- The `autosave.sav` file backs up your work every five minutes, so some work may be lost if Marigold closed due to a rare crash or accidental closure.
- To avoid accidentally closing Marigold with other browser tabs, consider installing Marigold as an "App" to keep it in its own window with a taskbar/desktop shortcut.
- Occasionally create versioned workspaces by clicking **Save** and appending a version number to the file name.

## Compositional Mapping Tool
URL: https://earthdaily.github.io/documentation/mining/using-marigold/compositional-mapping-tool-reko-diq/

# Hyperspectral Compositional Mapping Tool

## A step-by-step walkthrough using the Reko Diq porphyry system as a case study.

### Introduction

This tool was developed to help make characterization and quantification of hyperspectral mapping easier and more consistent for geologists who are not remote sensing experts. It includes assessment of mineral group relative abundance and compositional differentiation for selected mineral groups. The tool helps geologists build on multispectral compositional mapping results and derive quality analyses from hyperspectral data.

> **Note:** This tool does **not** work with multispectral (MS) data such as Sentinel-2, ASTER, or the Fused BEC.

The tool can be found under the **Hyperspectral** drop-down menu in the Marigold Mineral Processing Toolbox.

---

### Step 1 — Verify the Input Hyperspectral (HSI) Dataset

EnMap, PRISMA, EMIT, or other airborne/spaceborne hyperspectral datasets all work with the tool. The dataset should be cleaned using the **Data Statistical Analysis Tool** to remove bands with 0 or no-data values prior to use.

Typical input: `cleaned EnMap_Hyperspectral data [cEnMap_HSI]`

 

---

### Step 2 — Ensure All Required Masking Has Been Done

Typically includes masking for water, vegetation, cloud, cloud shadow, snow, ice, or topographic shadow.

Typical input: `[cEnMap_vwmsk_HSI]`

---

### Step 3 — Select the Compositional Mapping Tool

Under the Hyperspectral drop-down menu, select the compositional mapping tool. In the settings, select:

- Input dataset: `[cEnMap_vwmsk_HSI]`
- Base data name: e.g., `[CompMap_]`
- Mineral to be mapped: e.g., white mica

Results will be added to a `[CompMap – Outputs]` folder in the layer list.

---

### Step 4 — Examine Relative Abundance Results

Turn on the legend for each layer via the three-dot menu → **Toggle legend**. The default relative abundance is between ~0.2 (high, red) and ~0 (low, blue) using a `jet` color ramp.

**Key questions:**
- Does the distribution match what you know about mineral presence in the AOI?
- For white mica, do results correlate with the MS product `[AlOH_b6 index]`?
- Are the patterns and distribution consistent with the multispectral mapping?

Note that CompMap results include contributions from all white mica species (paragonite, muscovite, phengite, illite) and may also include other Al-OH species.

Adjust the min/max values for the histogram in the settings menu to better fit your data distribution.

---

### Step 5 — Examine Composition Results

For **white mica**, the primary compositions to differentiate are paragonite, muscovite, and phengite, defined by the wavelength position of the ~2200 nm absorption feature:

- **Paragonite**: ~2185–2195 nm
- **Muscovite**: ~2195–2210 nm
- **Phengite**: ~2210–2225 nm

The best way to validate is to spectrally verify the composition of a selection of pixels across the project area using the Spectral Query tool.

**Key questions:**
- Does the species identification match the predicted results?
- Are other minerals being mapped as white mica in lower relative abundance areas?
- Is there a positional relationship between paragonitic vs phengitic mica and mineralization (e.g., Au) or structural features?

## Hillshade and Variable Alpha
URL: https://earthdaily.github.io/documentation/mining/using-marigold/hillshade-and-variable-alpha/

# Hillshade and Variable Alpha Tools

## Marigold 2.8.0 introduces new hillshade and spatially variable alpha settings.

Hillshade visualizations can be a powerful tool to transform and interpret your data — not just for topography, but also for geophysical datasets and mineral processing maps.

**Jump to:** [Add a Raster Layer](#add-a-raster-layer) · [Generate a Hillshade](#generate-hillshade) · [Apply a Hillshade](#apply-hillshade) · [Spatially Variable Alpha](#spatially-varying-alpha) · [Additional Examples](#additional-examples)

### Add a Raster Layer

Click the `+` symbol next to Raster layers and select `Catalog layer`. Locate `Copernicus Digital Elevation Model (DEM) GLO-30` under the topography section, select it, and click `Add selected layers`.

The resulting view shows the elevation model with the default `gist_earth` colormap. Modify the appearance through the settings menu or click the magic wand button for an automatic 2% contrast stretch.

### Generate Hillshade

Expand `Raster management` in the processing toolbox and select `Generate Hillshade`. Select your input Product and the input band, then adjust the settings below.

#### Sun Angle

0–90°. Controls the height angle of the illumination source — 90° is directly overhead. Lower sun angles produce more shadows and may hide terrain features.

#### Sun Azimuth

0–360°. Controls the cardinal direction of the illumination source — 0° north, 90° east, 180° south, 270° west.

#### Z Factor

Vertical exaggeration — higher values produce more contrast and shadows between subtle features.

Once happy with the result, optionally rename your output layer and click `Create Hillshade`.

### Apply Hillshade

Enter the `settings` of any raster layer and scroll to `Advanced Settings`. Check the `Apply hillshade` box and select your generated hillshade product.

#### Blending Modes

Four pixel blending modes are available: **Multiply**, **Hard Light**, **Soft Light**, and **Linear Burn**. We recommend a trial and error approach. You will also find sliders for `Brightness` (−1 to +1) and `Gamma` (default 1).

### Spatially Varying Alpha

Within Advanced Settings, control the alpha (transparency) of the layer based on data values from a raster layer. This can be applied to the entire layer, or only to the applied hillshade layer.

### Additional Examples

#### Alteration Map of Goldfield, Nevada

A hillshade from the Copernicus DEM applied to the Alteration mineral index over Goldfield, Nevada adds important geological context — some strong alteration regions form resistive ridges, while others are more recessive.

#### Magnetic Structures in Kalgoorlie Gold District

A magnetic hillshade applied to the RGB True Color Composite of Sentinel-2 BEC over Kalgoorlie, Western Australia, juxtaposing complex magnetic features superimposed onto satellite imagery.

#### Airborne Magnetic and Radiometric Surveys — Republic, Washington

Using new airborne surveys from the USGS [EarthMRI initiative](https://www.usgs.gov/earth-mapping-resources-initiative-earth-mri). Topographic, magnetic, and radiometric hillshades are combined to outline magnetic highs associated with local Cretaceous plutons.

## Product Update Utility
URL: https://earthdaily.github.io/documentation/mining/using-marigold/product-utility-tool/

# Using the new Update Product Utility

## How to update a Marigold `.sav` file when access issues occur due to subscription changes.

When transitioning subscription levels in Marigold, users may face access challenges with layers due to unique Product IDs used for Bare Earth Composites in the EarthDaily Catalog. Before working with `.sav` files post-update, users must update the Product IDs of affected layers using the new `Update Computed Layer` tool.

> *NOTE: Tiles in Marigold will return this error when a user does not have access to the products required to create them.*

### Using the Tool

1. Open the file in Marigold
2. Navigate to the `Processing toolbox`
3. Click on the `Raster management` dropdown
4. Click on `Update computed layer`

Select all layers to create a comprehensive list of affected Product IDs. It is recommended to select all layers.

Choose the new Product ID to replace the old one. If a product does not require conversion, simply leave it blank.

Choose to either **Overwrite** (recommended) or **Append** the layers:

- **Overwrite**: The chosen layers will be recreated with updated product IDs, retaining their original names. The project is automatically resaved as `MarigoldProject_updated.sav`.
- **Append**: The selected layers will be duplicated with `_updated` appended to their names.

Click **Update Layers** and the utility will convert the layers and automatically save the file.

Your original `.sav` file will remain intact, allowing you to revert if needed.

Questions? Email [support@earthdaily.com](mailto:support@earthdaily.com).

## Spectral Query Tool
URL: https://earthdaily.github.io/documentation/mining/using-marigold/spectral-query-tool/

# The Spectral Query Tool

## Marigold 2.6.0 brings a totally revamped spectral query tool.

The spectral query tool forms the backbone of advanced spectral analysis in Marigold, serving as the primary point of pixel interrogation as you learn the properties of your area of interest. The 2.6.0 overhaul provides more precise control over point selection, query naming, band ranges, and source products.

### Using the New Spectral Query Tool

Select `Spectral Query` from the dropdown next to the `Spectra` section of the left-hand panel.

> **Note:** The spectra menu has been simplified to group features that either (1) add spectra to the project, or (2) export spectra from the project.

#### Selecting Products and Bands

The tool opens as a panel on the right-hand side. Select a spectral product and modify the band selection — for example, toggle only the VNIR bands from the Fused Bare Earth Composite, or select a range of bands from 2000–2500 nm from an EnMAP scene.

Click `Update band selection` to view an overlay of those bands on the spectrum plot below.

With the new tool, you can take repeated spectra measurements while the tool is open without permanently adding them to the project. Users can rename and change the color of each spectrum before saving.

#### Querying the Scene

Click `Start querying` to begin. Click points on the map to obtain the spectrum for that pixel. Continue clicking until you right-click the map to end the session.

Collect as many spectra as you like and compare them live on the spectral plot. You can even change the product and compare spectra from multiple sources at once.

#### Rename and Customize Your Selections

Once happy with your samples, rename and modify the line color of each spectrum and discard any you don't need. The checkbox toggle next to each spectrum name determines which samples will be saved. Click `Add selected spectra` to close the dialogue and add your spectra to the left-hand list.

## Vector Upload Guidelines
URL: https://earthdaily.github.io/documentation/mining/using-marigold/vector-upload-guidelines/

# Vector Upload Guidelines

## With the 2.6.0 update, Marigold now supports more vector upload formats and coordinate systems than ever.

### Supported Formats

Previous versions of Marigold exclusively supported vector uploads in `.geojson` format registered in WGS84 (`EPSG:4326`). With the new changes, vectors in most common formats and projections can now be uploaded directly without reformatting or reprojection.

> **Remember:** Vector formats that consist of multiple files, like ESRI Shapefiles and MapInfo Tab files, must be compressed into a `.zip` folder before uploading.

#### `.geojson`

- Fully compatible as a single `.geojson` file.
- Still requires exclusively `EPSG:4326` due to GeoJSON convention. Uploading in other coordinate systems may result in vectors appearing in the incorrect location.

#### `.shp`

- Must be uploaded in a `.zip` archive including all associated files (`.shx`, `.dbf`, `.prj`, `.cpg`, etc.).
- Most coordinate systems supported.

#### `.tab`

- MapInfo Tab files must be uploaded in a `.zip` archive including all associated files (`.dat`, `.id`, `.map`).
- Most coordinate systems supported.

#### `.gpkg`

- Highly flexible; can contain numerous spatial and non-spatial tables.
- Current support opens and displays the **first spatial table** in the `.gpkg` file. Separate complex files into individual tables prior to uploading.
- Most coordinate systems supported.

#### `.parquet`

- A relatively new and highly efficient vector table format.
- Exports from EarthDaily's Iris software for ground deformation monitoring are provided in `.parquet` format and can be uploaded directly.
- Most coordinate systems supported.

#### `.dxf`

- AutoCAD DXF files — a common output from 3D modeling software like Seequent's Leapfrog.
- Marigold is limited to 2D geographical coordinates; 3D DXF files may not behave as expected.
- Due to the nature of the DXF format, no CRS is provided. DXF files are only supported in `EPSG:4326`. Uploading in other coordinate systems may result in vectors appearing in the incorrect location.

# Marigold 2.12.0 — New Features

## The first major release of 2026 — compositional mapping expansion, raster calculator improvements, and a reinvented autosave.

### Compositional Mapping 2.0

In 2025 we released the first iteration of our advanced mineral and compositional mapping tool with White Mica capabilities. In this update, we expand the `Compositional Mapping` tool to include additional minerals key to hydrothermal mineral systems:

- **Chlorite**: abundance, MgO composition, FeO composition, and MgO/FeO ratio
- **Alunite**: abundance and composition
- **Kaolinite**: abundance
- **Carbonate**: abundance

The tool assesses the shape of the hull-normalized spectrum for each pixel and matches the presence or absence of key spectral absorption features to determine mineral presence. The **absorption depth slider** controls the threshold for matching a mineral based on the depth of the key absorption feature. Mineral composition is defined by the characteristics of that absorption feature — specifically the wavelength position of the minimum.

The workflow has been redesigned to be simpler: switch between minerals using the tabs at the bottom, modify the absorption depth minimum slider to adjust the threshold, and all outputs are added to a dedicated folder.

> The hyperspectral mineral abundance and composition mapping tool is powerful, but always use the spectral query tool to assess the effectiveness of mineral maps and refine your results.

---

### Mineral Indices Revamped

#### Raster Calculator: Store Your Favorite Formulas

The `Raster Calculator` now allows users to **save and name favorite expressions** for reuse across different sessions. Saved expressions can be loaded directly from the Raster Calculator interface and overwritten if needed. To delete saved expressions, find the full list in the `User Settings` panel, accessible from the Gear icon in the top ribbon.

> Remember: band names and wavelengths may be product-specific — a formula built using PRISMA wavelength positions won't work for EnMap imagery because the band positions differ.

#### Rapid Mapping Outputs Update

The Rapid Mapping Outputs tool is the top spot for EarthDaily's curated list of mineral indices, RGB band visualizations, and TRatio combinations specifically selected for mineral exploration. In this update:

- **Non-mineral indices** section added
- **Hyperspectral datasets** are now supported in addition to multispectral
- This tool will ultimately replace the original Mineral Indices and Hyperspectral Mineral Index Analogs tools

##### Non-mineral indices

New non-mineral indices added to help with masking residual features in hyperspectral imagery:

- NDVI — Vegetation Index
- NDWI — Water Index
- MNDWI — Modified Water
- NDSI — Snow Index
- NDGI — Glacier Index
- BSI — Bare Soil Index

##### Hyperspectral Analogs

The capabilities of the `Hyperspectral Mineral Analogs` tool have been moved into the Rapid Mapping Outputs tool. The tool finds the closest available hyperspectral band within 50 nm to match the required input band. If no band is within 50 nm, the index will not be available.

> Hyperspectral analogs should be interpreted with caution — they substitute a single narrow band for a much broader multispectral band and may not capture the full spectral response.

---

### Autosave Reinvented

The latest version of Marigold includes a **major update to the Autosave feature**:

- Any project saved with a name now has its own dedicated autosave version
- Autosaving triggers quietly every time a layer is created, in addition to the 10-minute checkpoints
- From the Load Project menu, any project saved with the new update shows an autosave checkpoint if available
- The name of the current project is displayed at the top of your Marigold session

> Note: Autosaving does not overwrite your main project file. Use the **Save** button to lock in changes, or **Save As** to create a divergent copy.

# Marigold 2.10.0 — New Features

## Hyperspectral capabilities take a huge leap forward with the initial release of the Compositional Mapping tool, alongside KMZ/KML upload, PCA improvements, and more.

### Compositional Mapping Tool

The new Compositional Mapping tool allows geologists to rapidly map the occurrence and composition of key minerals to build alteration models that advance exploration targets. In this first release, mapping capabilities are provided for three minerals associated with hydrothermal alteration:

- **White mica** — abundance and composition
- **Kaolinite** — abundance
- **Chlorite (MgOH)** — abundance

For a full walkthrough, see the [Hyperspectral Compositional Mapping Tool guide](../using-marigold/compositional-mapping-tool-reko-diq.md) and the [Marigold documentation](https://mining-energy-docs.appsci-production.earthone.earthdaily.com/#marigold/using-marigold/processes/comp-map-basic.html).

#### Mineral Abundance and Composition Theory

The tool works by analyzing each spectrum in the scene and looking for the presence or absence of absorption features that qualify or disqualify the pixel for a specific mineral. For example, to identify white mica, the tool looks for an absorption feature around 2200 nm and 2350 nm, while checking for the absence of a feature at 2160 nm (which would indicate kaolinite instead). Once identified as white mica, the composition is measured by mapping the wavelength position of the ~2200 nm absorption feature between paragonitic (~2180 nm), muscovitic (~2200 nm), and phengitic (~2219 nm).

#### Using the Tool

Load hyperspectral imagery from the Marigold Catalog — EnMap, PRISMA, EMIT, AVIRIS, or proprietary imagery. Open the `Compositional Mapping` tool under the `Hyperspectral` header in the `Processing Toolbox`.

> **Tip:** Outputs will be created in a folder system placed below any un-foldered layers. To visualize results above your imagery, add your source data to a folder before running the tool.

Select your source product, click your desired mineral, and results will be generated automatically. A sliding parameter filters the minimum absorption depth to tune the output. A higher threshold maps fewer pixels.

### Multiresolution Valley Bottom Flatness

Multiresolution Valley Bottom (and Ridgetop) Flatness (MRVBF and MRRTF) are algorithms that use DEMs to assess the flatness and lowness of terrain, identifying areas of valley bottom flatness that likely correlate to the depth of surficial deposits. Useful for geochemical sampling campaign design and hydrologic/geomorphologic applications.

Results are compiled into a unique Catalog product per DEM. The MRVBF product will have two bands: valley bottom flatness and ridge top flatness.

> Note: Only users with Compute Access will be able to use the MRVBF feature. Contact your organization administrator or EDA support with questions.
